A FEW GOOD QUESTIONS

Before your first step.

More detail is always there in the Ascend guide.

Do I need an account?

A private Ascend workspace opens its local learning account directly. Shared workspaces use a username and password; signup is available when the workspace administrator enables it.

Can I start as a beginner?

Yes. Look at a track’s entry skills and start with a worked example when a concept is new. Foundations and project tracks offer places to begin. If you already have experience, you can choose a later entry point based on what you can demonstrate.

What counts as progress?

You can save a partial learning session without completing a challenge. Completing a challenge requires its stated evidence. Verified XP follows recorded results; manual observations and practice have their own rules, explained in the guide.

How much time do I need?

Quick practice offers shorter activities, including ten-minute choices. Track challenges show their own estimates and may take longer. You can save partial work and return without changing the challenge’s finish line.

Do I need to install anything?

Worked examples and supported browser exercises open in your workspace. Some hands-on challenges need Python, a local Linux environment, containers, or your own devices. Read the setup and environment requirements before you start.

Is my learning progress private?

Learning histories are scoped to each account. Public streak sharing is optional and off by default. Your theme and optional remembered sign-in stay in your browser. Read how your data is stored.

YOUR WORK, YOUR CONTEXT

A clear view of
your data.

Ascend keeps your learning with your account. Here’s what this application stores and where it lives.

Learning data lives in this workspace.

Your tracks, notes, evidence, practice attempts, settings, and progress are stored in the Ascend installation’s SQLite database. In a private installation, that is your local learning account. A shared installation scopes learning records to the signed-in account.

The person operating this installation controls its hosting, backups, and database access. Contact them for account recovery, retention, export, or deletion requests.

Your browser remembers your preferences.

Theme and motion preferences are stored on this device. Browser recovery drafts retain their original account context. Sign-in credentials normally last for the tab’s session; choosing “Remember me” also stores your token in this browser for use after you close it. Sessions expire 30 days after sign-in and can be revoked.

Use your own browser profile on a shared device. Signing out ends the server session and clears browser credentials. Local recovery drafts may remain so unsaved work can be recovered.

Passwords and recovery codes are protected.

Passwords are stored as salted scrypt hashes. The server stores hashes of sign-in tokens and recovery codes. Self-service recovery uses a one-use code, replaces that code, and signs out existing sessions. Keep the code somewhere private; it can reset your password.

Sharing is a choice.

Public streak and badge sharing is off by default. Enabling it publishes limited streak statistics. The application does not include advertising or analytics scripts. Optional notification providers and learning-resource links have their own data practices when you use them.

API responses are never stored in the shared offline cache. The installed app caches its interface and documentation for offline reading; saving learning and signing in require a connection.
